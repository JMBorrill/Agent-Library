# Five Case Model and business case stages (Green Book 2026)

The source is Green Book 2026 para 3.12 and Table 2 (PDF p.20, printed p.14), plus Table 3 (PDF p.21, printed
p.15). The Green Book's main role is guidance on the **strategic** and **economic** cases (para 3.13). The
commercial, financial and management cases are covered in more depth by HM Treasury's business case guidance,
which is not bundled here.

The five cases are interconnected views of a single proposal, developed together and proportionately
(para 3.15). Do not invent extra "cases". Express any special feature, such as environmental, legal or ethical
considerations, as an objective, a CSF, or a social cost or benefit instead (para 3.16).

## What each case contains and must demonstrate (Table 2)

| Case | Must demonstrate | Contents listed in Table 2 | Guidance named in Table 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic | The rationale and objectives for the intervention | Case for change; theory of change; SMART objectives; business as usual; strategic fit | Business case guidance; Green Book Ch.4 |
| Economic | Longlist and shortlist of options for the strategic-case objectives, and the preferred option with the best value for money | Critical success factors; longlist appraisal; shortlist appraisal | Business case guidance; Green Book Chs.5-8 |
| Commercial | How government will source the inputs for the preferred option, and who manages delivery and operational risks | Procurement strategy; service requirements and outputs; risk allocation; charging mechanism; key contractual arrangements; personnel implications | Business case guidance; Commercial Standard; Sourcing Playbook; Cabinet Office Central Commercial Teams |
| Financial | Affordability for the public bodies involved | Public sector costs in RDEL, CDEL or AME; impact on income, expenditure and balance sheet; overall affordability and funding | Business case guidance; Consolidated Budgeting Guidance; departmental finance teams |
| Management | That the preferred option can be delivered successfully | Governance (roles, responsibilities, plans); change management, benefits realisation and risk management; M&E plan; contingency arrangements and plans | Business case guidance; Teal Book; Orange Book; Magenta Book |

Catalog ids: `case.strategic`, `case.economic`, `case.commercial`, `case.financial`, `case.management`.

## How the economic and financial cases differ (Table 9, PDF p.51)

| Aspect | Economic case | Financial case |
|---|---|---|
| Prices | Real (inflation removed) | Nominal |
| Discounting | Discounted at the STPR | Not discounted |
| Transfers | Included or excluded (explain which) | Always included |
| Wider social impacts | Included | Excluded |
| Depreciation | Not relevant (para 6.92) | Included |
| Contingency | Optimism bias adjustment sits in the costs | Remaining optimism bias plus unprevented risk, in nominal terms (para 6.84) |

## Business case stages (Table 3, PDF p.21)

| Stage | Purpose | Appraisal stages covered |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic Outline Case (SOC) | Makes the case for change, sets SMART objectives, and gives an initial longlist and shortlist to identify a preferred way forward | Rationale and objectives; options generation and longlist appraisal; identification of the preferred way forward |
| Outline Business Case (OBC) | Revisits the SOC options to identify the preferred option after detailed analysis, and outlines commercial, financial and management arrangements | Shortlist appraisal; identification of the preferred option |
| Full Business Case (FBC) | Sets out full commercial, financial and management arrangements after procurement | Review and iterate the appraisal after procurement |

Supplier engagement may bring new information that means the shortlist appraisal has to be revisited (para 6.3).
For local authority competitions, bidders work up to OBC, and the final allocation depends on a satisfactory FBC
with costs tied down (para 7.24, PDF p.58).

## Presentation (Chapter 9, PDF pp.75-77)

- Present spending appraisals in a business case that opens with an executive summary covering all five
  dimensions (para 9.2).
- Use the appraisal summary table (AST) for each shortlisted option (Table 12). It covers:
  - a description of the option, including why it is preferred or inferior;
  - summary metrics (NPSV, BCR and RPSC, or NPUC);
  - unmonetisable costs and benefits;
  - public sector financial impact (nominal, undiscounted);
  - distribution across groups and places;
  - risks and uncertainties for each of the above (paras 9.5-9.6).
- GMPP business cases must be published within **four months** of HM Treasury approval (para 10.12, PDF p.80).
