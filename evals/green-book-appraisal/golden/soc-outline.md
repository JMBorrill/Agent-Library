# Strategic Outline Case: North Shire Community Diagnostic Centre

Prepared in line with the HM Treasury Green Book 2026 (source id treasury-green) and the Five Case Model (para 3.12, Table 2). At SOC stage the economic analysis is indicative (para 5.10, Table 3).

## Executive summary

This SOC covers all five dimensions (para 9.2). It sets out the case for change, SMART objectives, a longlist assessed with the options framework filter, a shortlist of five options, and a preferred way forward to be tested at OBC.

## 1. Strategic case

### Case for change
Waiting times for diagnostic tests are rising across North Shire. The current hospital imaging capacity cannot meet projected demand. There is a co-ordination failure between providers, and households cannot provide this service themselves (paras 4.2-4.6).

### Theory of change
Inputs (staff, scanners, a building) produce outputs (additional scan capacity) that lead to outcomes: shorter waits, earlier diagnosis and better health. Each causal link has its assumptions and evidence recorded in the assumptions log (paras 4.9-4.13).

### Business as usual (BAU)
Under BAU, existing hospital imaging continues with planned replacement only. Waits are expected to grow by 15% a year, so BAU carries costs and risks of its own (paras 4.14-4.18).

### SMART objectives
1. Provide 60,000 additional diagnostic tests a year in North Shire by March 2029.
2. Bring 95% of patients to a test within six weeks of referral by March 2030.

### Strategic fit
The proposal fits the regional elective-recovery programme and the ICS estates strategy (para 4.27).

## 2. Economic case

### Critical success factors
CSF1 objectives and strategic fit; CSF2 value for money; CSF3 supplier capacity and capability; CSF4 affordability; CSF5 achievability (Table 4).

### Longlist and options framework filter
The longlist was generated in a facilitated workshop using the options framework filter. Option choices were rated for scope, solution, delivery, implementation and funding (paras 5.21-5.32). See the options long-list appraisal matrix in the annex, which records each red, amber or green rating with its rationale and source (para 5.27).

### Shortlist (around five options, paras 5.17-5.18)
1. Business as usual
2. Do minimum: extend hospital imaging opening hours
3. Preferred way forward: a new community diagnostic centre on an existing NHS site
4. More ambitious: a new centre plus two spoke sites
5. Less ambitious: a modular, leased centre

### Indicative appraisal and preferred way forward
Indicative costs and benefits are in real terms and discounted at the STPR (3.5% for years 1-30). Health effects are discounted at 1.5% (paras 6.54, 6.58), and costs include optimism bias adjustments taken from user-supplied supplementary guidance values. On this basis the preferred way forward has the highest indicative NPSV (about £40m to £70m). It is the preferred way forward because it best meets the CSFs at proportionate risk. The preferred option will be confirmed at OBC through full shortlist appraisal, sensitivity analysis and switching values, and an appraisal summary table. No BCR threshold is applied (para 6.98).

## 3. Commercial case
The procurement strategy will test the market for modular and conventional construction. The case will also cover service requirements, risk allocation, the charging mechanism and personnel implications (Table 2).

## 4. Financial case
Indicative capital of £35m (CDEL) and running costs of £6m a year (RDEL), in nominal terms. Affordability within the ICS capital envelope is to be confirmed. Contingency is informed by the remaining optimism bias (para 6.83).

## 5. Management case
- Governance: SRO, programme board and the gateway review plan.
- Benefits realisation: a benefits register owned by named benefit owners.
- Risk management: a risk register following the Orange Book.
- Monitoring and evaluation plan: an outline M&E plan based on the theory of change, with M&E costs included (paras 10.7-10.8).
